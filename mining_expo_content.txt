WHAT THE EXPO IS (from criteria.md, Section 6)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- All groups present at the end-of-semester Mining Expo.
- Every group applied the SAME 5-phase KDD methodology to a DIFFERENT banking
  dataset, so the expo is structured ground for cross-group comparison.
- Format context: the graded presentation is 10 minutes, and the rubric rewards
  structure, clarity, and the team demonstrating full understanding under
  questioning (Section 5.5).
- The expo's message: mining techniques are domain-agnostic, but the MEANING of
  discovered knowledge is always shaped by context.

The four questions every group must address:
  Q1. Which association rules were the most surprising, and why?
  Q2. Which clustering method produced the most interpretable segments?
  Q3. What anomalies were found, and what do they suggest in a real banking context?
  Q4. How do your findings compare to what another group discovered in a
      different banking domain?

SLIDE 1 — Q1: MOST SURPRISING ASSOCIATION RULES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Headline: The bank is losing SENIORS with shallow relationships — and Germany
compounds everything.

Rule table (all consequent = Churned; baseline churn 20.4%):
  #1 MOST SURPRISING: {Inactive AND Senior AND 1 Product}
     Support 4.05% | Confidence 77.3% | Lift 3.79
     Why surprising: inactivity alone is a much weaker signal — the risk EXPLODES
     only when inactivity intersects senior age (46-60) and single-product depth.
     ~4 in 5 of these customers left. Simple cross-tabs never show this.

  #2 {Senior AND Germany}
     Support 3.38% | Confidence 67.3% | Lift 3.31
     Why surprising: German seniors churn at >3x baseline REGARDLESS of activity
     or product count. Suggests a structural product-fit or service issue in the
     German market, not just an age effect. Extra credibility: Geography was NOT
     used in clustering distance, so this is not a modeling artifact.

  #3 ASSIGNED HYPOTHESIS — CONFIRMED: {Germany AND Inactive AND 1 Product}
     Support 3.75% | Confidence 52.1% | Lift 2.56
     The project's assigned hypothesis ("inactive German single-product customers
     are a strong churn profile") is validated at 2.5x baseline.

Supporting depth (if asked): 10 rules documented (A-J), thresholds justified
(support >= 0.03 ~ 300 customers, confidence >= 0.50, lift >= 1.5); credit card
ownership does NOT protect single-product seniors (lift ~2.9); the bank retains
wealthy seniors but loses MID-balance seniors (50-125K, lift ~2.8).

Key takeaway line for the slide:
"Churn risk is an INTERACTION effect: age x engagement x product depth.
No single attribute tells this story."

SLIDE 2 — Q2: MOST INTERPRETABLE CLUSTERING METHOD
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Headline: K-Means (K=3) gave the most interpretable personas; DBSCAN earned its
keep elsewhere — as an anomaly detector.

Answer: K-MEANS with K=3.

Evidence for K choice (both metrics, honestly interpreted):
- Elbow: inertia falls smoothly 54.4K (K=2) -> 31.8K (K=12), no sharp kink;
  marginal gains shrink steadily after K=4.
- Silhouette: peaks at K=2 (0.164), K=3 = 0.150 — weak global structure.
- Decision: K=3 chosen INSIDE the defensible K=2-4 range because it yields the
  most interpretable personas without over-splitting. (Be upfront: these are
  descriptive personas, not sharply separated natural populations.)

The three personas (named ONLY from dimensions that statistically separate them —
Balance eps^2=0.727, NumOfProducts eps^2=0.713, Geography V=0.327):
  C0 "High-Balance Multi-Product Germany-Skew Mixed-Risk"
     2,169 customers (21.7%) | churn 21.8% | 52% German | 2.13 products avg
  C1 "High-Balance Single-Product France-Skew Watchlist"
     4,168 customers (41.7%) | churn 25.6% | 100% single-product | THE watchlist
  C2 "Zero-Balance Multi-Product France-Skew Loyalist"
     3,663 customers (36.6%) | churn 13.6% | 98.4% zero balance | Germany ~absent

Cross-checks (shows method understanding):
- Ward hierarchical: silhouette 0.128 but ARI 0.746 / NMI 0.701 agreement with
  K-Means -> confirms the same broad structure. Dendrograms compared across
  single / complete / average / ward linkages.
- DBSCAN (eps=1.25 via k-NN graph): poor for personas, but its 554 noise points
  churn at 62.6% — repurposed as the Phase 4 structural anomaly source.

Key takeaway line:
"K-Means named the segments; DBSCAN found the strangers. Using each method for
what it is actually good at IS the discovery skill."

SLIDE 3 — Q3: ANOMALIES FOUND + REAL BANKING MEANING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Headline: The riskiest customers are not extreme on ANY single value — they are
unusual COMBINATIONS of normal values.

Detection scoreboard (churn rate among flagged; baseline 20.4%):
  IQR (univariate)               374 flagged | 23.5% churn
  Z-score (univariate)           141 flagged | 13.5% churn  <- BELOW baseline!
  Isolation Forest (multivar.)   500 flagged | 49.0% churn
  DBSCAN noise (Phase 2)         554 flagged | 62.6% churn  <- strongest detector
  Robust Mahalanobis (extension) 142 flagged | 58.5% churn
  LOF (extension)                500 flagged | 32.4% churn

Headline discovery (multivariate extension):
- 580 records are flagged ONLY by multivariate methods; 90% of them have NO
  single feature beyond |z|=3. They churn at 45.3% — the highest of all segments.
- Meaning: single-feature monitoring dashboards structurally CANNOT see the
  bank's riskiest customers. Risk is relational, not marginal.

Cross-reference with Phase 2 (explicitly required, done):
- IF vs DBSCAN agreement: 336 shared records, Jaccard 0.468, kappa 0.617 —
  two different structural paradigms converging on the same unusual customers.

Anomaly typology (876 flagged records classified with evidence):
  A: Data Error          2 (0.2%)  -> data-quality review only
  B: Rare Valid        468 (53.4%) -> e.g., young high-balance customers,
                                      4-product holders; monitor, do NOT delete
  C: Risk Signal       406 (46.3%) -> 221 density-outlier churners,
                                      136 high-balance pre-churn (avg ~150K,
                                      46% German), 44 disengaged single-product,
                                      5 senior high-value departures

Real banking context (say this out loud):
- High-balance pre-churn cases are the most financially painful: modest churn
  LIFT but very large money walking out; needs relationship-manager outreach.
- Rare-valid cases prove anomaly does not equal problem: half of all flags are
  legitimate unusual customers; deleting them would erase discovery value.

SLIDE 4 — Q4: CROSS-GROUP COMPARISON
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Headline: Same pipeline, different domain, different KNOWLEDGE — churn is about
relationship depth; fraud/credit risk are about capacity and transactions.

Our domain's signature (prepared position):
- In churn, ENGAGEMENT and RELATIONSHIP-DEPTH variables dominate discovery
  (Age, NumOfProducts, IsActiveMember, Geography, Balance), while classic
  financial-capacity variables (CreditScore, EstimatedSalary) are nearly
  useless — near-zero correlation AND near-zero information gain.

Ready-made contrasts (fill in the other group's actual numbers at the expo):
- vs FRAUD (Groups 3/8): fraud anomalies are adversarial and hidden by intent;
  our churn anomalies are behavioral and hidden by dimensionality. Both groups
  likely found that the interesting records are combination-anomalies — compare
  their fraud flags with our 580 multivariate-only pool (45.3% churn).
- vs CREDIT RISK / MORTGAGE (e.g., Group 4): their discovery variables should be
  exactly the capacity variables (income, credit score, LTV) that are DEAD in
  our dataset. Same algorithms, inverted variable importance — the cleanest
  possible proof that meaning is domain-shaped.
- vs AML: AML flags network/velocity patterns across transactions; we flag
  cross-sectional profile patterns. Both rely on "unusual combination" logic
  rather than single-threshold rules.
- Shared methodology lesson to state: every group had to discretize, tune
  support/confidence thresholds, and defend K — the pipeline is domain-agnostic,
  the knowledge is not.

SLIDE 5 — CENTRAL KDD ANSWER (CLOSER)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
"What did we discover that was not already obvious from the raw data?"
1. Churn risk is an interaction profile, not an attribute: inactive senior
   single-product customers churn at 77% (3.8x baseline).
2. Germany is a structural retention problem, not a demographic quirk — and it
   emerged POST-clustering, so it is a discovered pattern, not an artifact.
3. The 41.7% of the book that is high-balance + single-product is the true
   watchlist segment (25.6% churn) — invisible in any single-column view.
4. The riskiest anomalies are combinations of individually normal values
   (45.3% churn, 90% with no |z|>3) — unusualness in this book is relational,
   not marginal.
5. Anomaly does not mean error: 53% of flagged records are rare-but-legitimate
   customers worth keeping.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ANTICIPATED EXPO QUESTIONS + PREPARED ANSWERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Q: "Your silhouette is only 0.15 — aren't your clusters meaningless?"
A: Weak global separation is reported honestly; K=3 is a descriptive persona
   solution chosen inside the defensible K=2-4 range. Separation is real on the
   dimensions that matter (Balance eps^2=0.73, Products eps^2=0.71) and the
   personas are validated by an external lens (churn 13.6% vs 25.6%).

Q: "Why is the churn label in the data if this isn't prediction?"
A: Used strictly as a post-hoc validation lens for profiling — never as a
   supervised target. No classifier, no accuracy metric anywhere.

Q: "Why did you add Mahalanobis/LOF when the task only asks IQR/Z/IF?"
A: IQR/Z are univariate; comparing them against a multivariate family let us
   decompose WHY methods disagree — which produced the single strongest finding
   of Phase 4. Extension beyond requirements, not replacement.

Q: "Class C risk signals churn at 100% — isn't that circular?"
A: Yes, by design and disclosed: the typology uses observed churn as supporting
   evidence to CLASSIFY flagged anomalies, not to predict. The forward-looking
   value is the profile definition, applied to still-active customers.

Q: "Why lift and not just confidence?"
A: Confidence inherits the base rate; lift normalizes by it. A 77% confidence
   rule matters because baseline is 20.4% (lift 3.79), not because 77% is big.
